# Positioning

Who Ravelston is for and what it beats. Supports [[GTM Strategy]] — mostly a
set of hypotheses to test in [[Customer Discovery]], not conclusions.

## The claim

Every lifting tracker on the market measures how the bar *moved*. You tell it
what was on the bar. Ravelston measures what's on the bar.

If the bar-whip load estimation in [FINDINGS.md](../../../research/FINDINGS.md)
holds up, that is a category difference, not a feature difference — and it's
the only defensible thing here, since ROM, rep counting, and rest timing are
all commodity IMU features that a dozen products already do.

The corollary: **if Stage 0 fails, this note is void** and Ravelston is a
late entrant in a crowded ROM/technique market. Worth re-reading the moment
real bar data exists.

## Adjacent categories

Ravelston is not really in any of these, which is the point, but buyers will
compare against them:

- **VBT sensors** (IMU or linear-position transducer devices — Enode,
  Vitruve, Output Sports, GymAware, RepOne, and the discontinued Beast
  Sensor and PUSH Band). Measure bar velocity for autoregulation. Coach-led
  market, established, and the segment that already spends money on this
  class of hardware. All of them require weight entry.
- **Camera / computer-vision systems** (Perch and similar) — facility-
  installed, no attachment friction, but fixed to a rack and priced for
  institutions.
- **App loggers** (Hevy, Strong, Boostcamp, etc.) — the actual incumbent for
  individual lifters. Free or cheap, no hardware, and the thing most people
  will be "switching" from. The real competitor for a consumer segment is
  not a rival sensor, it's a notes app that costs nothing.
- **Smartwatches and connected gym machines** — set-and-rep guesswork or
  machine-side data. Adjacent, mostly irrelevant.

**All prices, product statuses, and feature claims above are from memory
and unverified.** Before this note informs any decision, do a proper
competitive sweep and record dates and sources — the VBT market has churned
hard (Beast and PUSH both died) and stale competitor data is worse than
none.

## Hypotheses to test in G0

Each of these is a belief that would change the product if false:

1. **Not knowing the weight is a felt problem** — unmarked dumbbells,
   machines with no numbers, plate math, forgetting to log. If interviews
   don't surface this unprompted, the core differentiator has no demand
   behind it.
2. **Automatic logging beats manual logging enough to justify hardware** —
   people already tolerate typing numbers into an app. The bar to clear is
   not "is it easier", it's "is it worth £X and remembering to charge it".
3. **Two sensors on the bar is acceptable friction** — attaching hardware
   every session, twice. Coaches may accept more friction than individuals;
   individuals may accept none.
4. **Symmetry and tilt are wanted, not just measurable** — the second
   sensor's unique outputs. If nobody values them, the second sensor is pure
   cost and the product story simplifies.
5. **The buyer is the individual lifter, not the coach** — this determines
   price, channel, and whether the product is one pair or a roster of pairs.
   Currently a coin flip; G0 exists to settle it.

## Provisional segment bet

**Serious individual lifters who follow a program** — they log, they care
about numbers, and they train in commercial gyms full of unlabelled
equipment. The coach/facility market is more proven and better funded but is
a slower sales motion with feature expectations set by $1k+ incumbents.

This is a bet, not a decision. Revisit after G0.
