Ravelston · mounting research round 2

Ten mount options, ranked

Requirements: works on plastic and metal · instant on/off · small and simple · bar-end mounting allowed (rotation accepted). From 188 sources across two research waves. Full analysis lives in research/MOUNTING.md; each option links to the real product it's patterned on.

  1. Snap cap over the sleeve endrecommended combo

    bar only<1 s push-clickno moving parts~$1–2/unit

    A rigid "pen cap" that clicks over the standardized Ø50 mm sleeve end — a full-circle snap ridge is the strongest small fastener there is. Spins with the sleeve (now fine). Patent-clear: grips the exterior, never the bore.

    50 mm pipe snap clamps · Ø50 slide-over caps

  2. Quick-Fist-style rubber C-clamprecommended combo

    plastic ✓1–2 s one-handedØ25–57 mm rangevibration-proven product

    One piece of molded rubber: the C flexes over the sleeve or dumbbell head, a stretch tab pulls over a knob. No lever, no hinge, no spring. The real product is sold for bolting tools to off-road trucks — 25 lb working load. The node's own housing becomes the clamp.

    Quick Fist Original · Quick Fist Mini

  3. Mini spring-jaw claw

    plastic ✓~2 s squeezewidest range — even Bowflex shellsmost mechanism

    A shrunken GoPro Jaws: squeeze-open padded jaws + small locking plunger. The only single mechanism spanning sleeve rim, hex head, and a fat plastic adjustable. The consumer version is 9× too heavy — miniaturize the idea, not the product.

    GoPro Jaws Flex Clamp · 3-jaw oil-filter gripper

  4. Silicone stretch boot

    round plastic heads ✓2–4 s on / 1 s offsoft coupling — whip-signal risk

    A silicone sock stretched over the sleeve end — Ø50 slide-over caps are already a commodity. Zero hardware, spins with the sleeve. The elastomer between sensor and bar is the physics caveat; hex and Bowflex heads defeat it.

    Food Huggers stretch lids · stretch-lid size guide

  5. Split system: cheap carrier + quarter-turn puckrecommended combo

    plastic ✓ (via carrier)~1 s node swapthe system pattern

    The Garmin bike-computer trick: a $1–2 passive carrier lives on (or clips to) each equipment type, and the sensor puck twists in and out in one second. The AirTag accessory industry converged on the same puck-vs-carrier split. Carriers = option 1 for barbells, option 2 for dumbbells.

    Garmin quarter-turn mount · CatEye FlexTight

  6. Mini cam-lever C-collar

    drop-proven geometry~2 shas a leverbar + handles only

    A Lock-Jaw barbell collar shrunk to ~20 mm wide with a sensor pocket. The collar industry has already proven this exact clamp survives repeated bar drops at Ø50. Stiffest option of all — but it's the lever-y design language you didn't like.

    Lock-Jaw collar · Lock-Jaw Pro

  7. Magnet + Velcro One-Wrap combo

    <1 s on steeltwo different gesturesmagnet sheds under vibration

    What OpenBarbell/Enode users do today: rubber-faced magnet for steel, velcro wrap for everything else. Works, ships, boring — but you carry two attachment modes and magnets have documented vibration failures.

    Enode Pro (magnetic) · Velcro One-Wrap

  8. Knog-style stretch tail + nub

    plastic ✓~2 ssoftest of the tension family

    The molded silicone loop-and-nub from the previous concept page. Still legitimate — just outranked by the clamps on stiffness and by the snap cap on speed.

    Knog Big Cobber · Knog Plus mount

  9. Gecko dry-adhesive pad

    smooth surfaces onlychalk degrades itweak in peel under drops

    The only "sticky" survivor: 30–40 N/cm² shear on smooth chrome or glossy plastic, stiff and residue-free. But it needs a wipe ritual, fails on textured rubber, and a drop's peel component pops it off. Niche bench-test candidate.

    Setex gecko tapes · OnRobot Gecko gripper

  10. Molded rubber twist-tail

    plastic ✓5–8 s, two handswire core fatigues

    A Gear-Tie-style bendable tail molded into the node: wrap and twist. Universal but the slowest gesture here and the wire work-hardens over hundreds of cycles. Last resort.

    Nite Ize Gear Tie

The recommendation: options 1 + 2 unified by option 5 — a quarter-turn puck node, a snap cap as its barbell carrier (push-click onto the Ø50 sleeve end), and a rubber C-clamp as its dumbbell carrier (plastic shells included). Every gesture ≤2 s, nothing folds or ratchets, one node design. Gate: the sleeve-spin bench test must confirm whip fidelity through the bearing before committing to end-mounting — if it fails, the C-clamp moves to the shaft inside the collars and everything else stands.

Why nothing sticky made the list

Chalk dust is a granular lubricant on exactly the smooth surfaces adhesives need; texture (rubber/plastic at tens of microns) collapses gecko-class adhesion by orders of magnitude; and drop shock always finds the weak peel direction — suction cups fail it instantly and completely. Squeeze-based geometry is immune to all three. Details and 188 sources: research/MOUNTING.md.

Round 3 — ten novel mechanisms (all barbell + dumbbell)

From three fresh sweeps: how other sports' sensors attach, aerospace/industrial fasteners, and everyday objects & biology (68 new sources). The meta-pattern from sports tech: successful sensors occupy an existing standardized feature — and dumbbells turn out to have one (the recessed head bolt, dead-center = ideal IMU spot).

  1. Spring claw with interleaving rubber teethstrongest single SKU

    one-hand, <1 sØ50 sleeve AND 60–180 mm heads, one partany material

    A hair claw clip crossed with a guitar trigger capo: torsion-spring jaws whose interleaving teeth self-adjust to whatever size they close around. Claw clips survive all day on a moving head; capos hold tapered necks through hours of string vibration.

    claw clip mechanics · trigger capo patent

  2. Press-in dock + twist-lock nodestrongest system

    ~2 s node hopdock installs once per equipmentSony shipped this for 100+ rackets

    The Sony/Zepp tennis pattern: a cheap dock replaces the barbell sleeve's press-fit end plug, and presses or screws into the dumbbell's head-bolt recess; the sensor quarter-twists between docks. Zero per-session friction. (Sleeve-plug version needs a patent check vs Eleiko's in-cavity claims.)

    Sony adapter ring · Zepp insert mount

  3. Windlass strap (tourniquet mechanism)

    any diameter, one SKUhuge tension, flick releasebattlefield-proven

    Flat band + stubby twist rod that clicks into a keeper. Unlimited take-up means the same part fits a sleeve, a hex head, or a kettlebell handle. Combat tourniquets prove it works in seconds under the worst conditions imaginable.

    NAR CAT tourniquet

  4. Eversion cuff over an undercut flange

    3–5 scapture, not frictionsurvives full-speed bat swings

    Blast Motion's bat-knob trick: turn a silicone lip inside-out, seat the sensor, roll the lip back so it swallows the flange. Works on the sleeve's rim step and a dumbbell's head-to-handle step.

    Blast bat mount

  5. Instrumented "smart bolt"

    zero per-session attachdead-center = ideal IMU spotcoverage varies by equipment

    Garmin's golf pattern: replace an existing recessed fastener with an instrumented twin — the head screw on most fixed dumbbells, the end bolt on bolt-construction bars. Set once, forget forever.

    Garmin CT10

  6. Garter-spring boot rim

    push-on/pull-offfixes silicone-boot creeplip-seal technology

    A circular coil spring molded into a push-on boot's rim — the exact mechanism that keeps lip seals preloaded on rotating shafts for millions of revolutions. Upgrades the stretch boot from "fine for a week" to seal-grade sustained grip.

    garter spring design

  7. Rubber over-center T-hook cradle

    one molded partno leverengine-hood latch DNA

    A TPU T-loop stretches 3–5 mm over a horn into a defined preloaded "hooked" state — over-center by rubber geometry alone. Hood latches prove it against engine-bay vibration and shock.

    rubber hood fastener

  8. Releasable ratchet tie

    zip-tie fast250 lb strappinch-release under tension

    SpeedyTie-style ratchet channel molded into the node, serrated strap wraps anything, 1 mm-pitch take-up, one-hand release. The cheapest credible mechanism found in any round.

    HellermannTyton SpeedyTie

  9. Remora pad (suction + micro-spinule hybrid)

    loves rough + compliant surfaceshighest R&D costScience Robotics, 340× body weight

    The one adhesion-family survivor: lamellae suction chambers plus ~1000 carbon-fiber micro-spinules that mechanically grab surface asperities — it holds best exactly where gyms defeat plain suction and gecko.

    remora disc paper

  10. Edge-hooking fold-over claws

    grips an edge, not a diametersize- and finish-agnostic

    Violin shoulder-rest feet: rubber-over-steel fold-over claws that hook an edge — the sleeve-end lip, a dumbbell head's step — and hold through hours of played vibration without marking varnish.

    Kun shoulder rest · US5419226A

Honorable mentions that failed the barbell-AND-dumbbell filter: the cufflink T-bar twisted into the plate-hub gap (brilliant, bar-only), log-spiral climbing cams into the sleeve bore (self-tightening, but bore geometry varies + patent risk), aerospace ball-lock pins and LEGO-clutch precision as the node↔carrier docking standard, and bird-tendon micro-ridges (grip engages under load, releases when lifted) as a texture to add to any jaw face.

The wildcard shelf

The "just stick it on" staircase (full write-up in research/MOUNTING.md): ① magnet-primary v1 with a strap fallback — shippable now, covers the ~90% of commercial-gym equipment that is steel; ② the tri-hybrid pad — one Ø50 mm disc combining a pot magnet (steel), a gecko-tape ring (smooth plastic), and a microspine rim (rough rubber/iron), press-and-twist to lock — nobody has built it, pairwise research precedents only, falsifiable with a $30 franken-pad test; ③ the remora pad (option 9 above) as the R&D endgame.

Other ideas kept alive from the creative round: a magnetorheological pad (fluid pouch that rigidizes over a magnet — conforms to any texture, then locks stiff), a self-cinching twisted-string lasso (HyperAdapt-style, firmware-released), and the sensor-as-a-change-plate. The MR pad attacks precisely the failure modes that killed adhesives.
